How to write an invoice as a subcontractor in Australia

Updated 19 October 2026

If you work on an ABN, you get paid when you send an invoice. A clear, correct invoice gets paid faster and keeps you out of trouble at tax time. Here is exactly what to put on it.

What must be on your invoice

  1. Your name or business name, as registered.
  2. Your ABN. Without it, the business paying you may have to withhold 47% of the payment and send it to the ATO.
  3. The date you issue the invoice.
  4. A unique invoice number. Simple works best: 1, 2, 3… Never reuse a number.
  5. Who you are billing: the client’s business name, and their ABN if you have it.
  6. What the work was: the dates, the site, the hours and the rate.
  7. The total amount in Australian dollars.
  8. GST: only if you are registered for GST (see below).
  9. How and when to pay you: account name, BSB, account number and the due date.

Are you registered for GST?

This changes two things on your invoice.

  • Not registered: call it an Invoice, do not add GST, and it is good practice to write “No GST has been charged”.
  • Registered: call it a Tax Invoice, add 10% GST and show the GST amount (or state that the total includes GST). For a tax invoice of $1,000 or more, include the buyer’s name or ABN.

How to bill weekly hours

Many subbies are paid by the hour, weekly or fortnightly. List each day on its own line so your client can check it quickly:

DateHoursRateAmount
Mon 12/108.5$45.00$382.50
Tue 13/108.5$45.00$382.50
Wed 14/108.0$45.00$360.00
Thu 15/108.5$45.00$382.50
Fri 16/107.5$45.00$337.50
Total41.0$1,845.00
  • Take out unpaid breaks. 7:00am to 3:30pm with a 30-minute unpaid lunch is 8 hours, not 8.5.
  • Show higher rates on their own line, for example overtime after 8 hours, Saturdays or public holidays, if your agreement includes them.
  • Add the site address if you work on several jobs for the same client.

Paid by the day or a fixed price?

  • Day rate: one line per day worked, with the day rate, for example “Mon 12/10 · 1 day · $350.00”.
  • Fixed weekly amount: one line with the week and the agreed amount, for example “12/10 – 18/10 · Weekly services (fixed rate) · $1,800.00”. No need to list hours.
  • Fixed price for a job: one line with the dates, a clear description of the job and the agreed price.

The rest of the invoice does not change: your ABN, the client, a unique number, the dates, GST if you are registered, and how to pay you.

Payment terms

Agree on terms with your client before you start, and write them on every invoice. Common terms are 7 or 14 days. Put a clear due date, for example “Due 23/10/2026”, rather than only “14 days”.

Mistakes that delay payment

  • Missing or wrong ABN.
  • Wrong BSB or account number. Double-check them once and save them.
  • The same invoice number used twice.
  • Adding GST when you are not registered. You are not allowed to charge it.
  • Hours that don’t match the client’s timesheets. Keep your own record every day.

Keep your records

Keep a copy of every invoice you send. The ATO generally requires you to keep business records for 5 years. In the building and construction industry, the businesses that pay contractors also report those payments to the ATO each year, so make sure all your invoices are included in your tax return.

Quick answers

Can I send an invoice without an ABN?

You can, but the business paying you may have to withhold 47% of the amount for the ATO. If you work as a subcontractor, get an ABN first. It’s free at abr.gov.au.

How should I number my invoices?

Any system works as long as every number is unique and in order. Starting at 1 and counting up is perfectly fine.

How often should I invoice?

Match your client’s pay cycle: weekly, fortnightly or monthly. Sending on time usually means getting paid on time.
