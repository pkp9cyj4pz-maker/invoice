Free timesheet template for subcontractors

Updated 20 October 2026

Your invoice is only as good as your hours. Write them down every day, on the day, and invoicing becomes a two-minute job. Here is a simple timesheet that works on any site.

What to record each day

  1. Date and day of the week.
  2. Site or job address, especially if you work on several jobs for the same client.
  3. Start time and finish time.
  4. Unpaid break, for example a 30-minute lunch.
  5. Hours worked: finish minus start, minus unpaid breaks.
  6. Notes: overtime, a public holiday, travel, or who asked you to stay back.

Converting minutes to decimal hours

Invoices use decimal hours, so 8 hours 30 minutes is 8.5, not 8.30. The common ones:

MinutesDecimal
15 min0.25
30 min0.5
45 min0.75

Example: 7:00 to 15:45 with a 30-minute unpaid break is 8 h 45 min − 0 h 30 min = 8 h 15 min, so 8.25 hours.

Tips that save arguments

  • Fill it in on the day. Memory on Friday afternoon is not a record.
  • Match the client’s timesheets. If they keep their own sign-in sheet, check yours against it each week.
  • Keep it with the invoice. If a client questions an hour, you can show the day it came from.
  • Keep your records. The ATO generally requires business records to be kept for 5 years.

Quick answers

Do I need my client to sign my timesheet?

It isn’t required by law, but many builders ask for it, and a signed timesheet makes disputes rare. Ask your client what they want before you start.

Should I round my hours?

Agree on it with your client. Many round to the nearest 15 minutes. Whatever you choose, do it the same way every day.
