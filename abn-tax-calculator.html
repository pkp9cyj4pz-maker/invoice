How much tax do I pay on ABN income?

Updated 28 October 2026 · 2026–27 rates

When you work under an ABN, nobody takes tax out of your pay. You get the full amount, and you pay the tax yourself at the end of the financial year. Here is how to work out how much, and how much to put aside as you go.

Step 1: work out your taxable income

Add up what you invoiced during the financial year (1 July to 30 June), without the GST. Then take away the work expenses you can claim. What is left is your taxable income. If you also had a job with a salary, that counts too.

Example: you invoiced $60,000 and spent $4,000 on tools, safety gear and the work part of your phone. Your taxable income is $56,000.

There is no tax-free threshold: you pay 15% from the first dollar. This applies to ABN income too, not only to wages.

Foreign residents

30% up to $135,000, then 37% up to $190,000 and 45% above. No tax-free threshold and no Medicare levy.

Step 3: don’t forget GST

If you are registered for GST, the 10% you add to your invoices is not your money. You pay it to the ATO in your Business Activity Statement (BAS), usually every quarter. Put all of it aside, on top of what you keep for income tax. Not registered? Then there is no GST to pay. See Do subcontractors charge GST?

How much should I put aside?

The safest habit is to move money every time you get paid. For most subbies, 25% to 30% of what you earn before GST covers income tax and the Medicare levy with a bit of margin. If you’re registered for GST, also put aside all of the GST you charge. A separate savings account makes it easy not to touch it.

When and how you pay

  • Tax return: lodge it after 30 June through myGov (ATO online) by 31 October, or later if you use a registered tax agent. The ATO then sends you the bill.
  • PAYG instalments: after your first year with business income, the ATO may ask you to pay part of next year’s tax each quarter. It is credited when you lodge.
  • BAS: only if you are registered for GST. Usually every quarter.

Quick answers

Is tax taken out of my ABN payments?

No. When you work under an ABN, your client pays you the full amount. Nobody withholds tax for you, so you pay it yourself when you lodge your tax return after 30 June.

How much of my ABN income should I put aside for tax?

A common rule of thumb is 25% to 30% of what you earn before GST, plus every dollar of GST you charge if you are registered. Use the calculator above to get a figure for your own income.

When do I pay the tax on my ABN income?

The financial year runs from 1 July to 30 June. You lodge your tax return after 30 June (by 31 October if you do it yourself, later if you use a registered tax agent) and the ATO sends you a bill for the tax you owe.

What are PAYG instalments?

Once you have earned business income, the ATO may ask you to pay part of next year's tax in advance every quarter. These are PAYG instalments. They are credited against your tax bill when you lodge.

Do working holiday makers pay a different rate on ABN income?

Yes. On a 417 or 462 visa, your working holiday income is taxed at 15% from the first dollar up to $45,000, with no tax-free threshold, then at the normal higher rates.

What can I claim as a deduction?

Costs you have for your work: tools and equipment, safety gear and work clothing, the work part of your phone and internet, travel between job sites, and tax agent fees. Keep the receipts.
